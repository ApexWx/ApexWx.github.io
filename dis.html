About Apex Wx

Apex Wx is an independent, non-governmental weather information website primarily focused on Fort Kent, the Upper Saint John Valley, and surrounding areas of northern Maine. The site provides weather forecasts, discussions, observations, and related meteorological information for general public use.

Apex Wx is not an official meteorological agency or government weather service. Its forecasts and discussions represent independent interpretations of available meteorological data and guidance.

Website Author and Forecasting Background

Apex Wx is developed and maintained by Joseph E. Becker, a professor at the University of Maine at Fort Kent, a graduate of Penn State University's weather forecasting certificate program, and a National Weather Service-trained storm spotter.

His meteorological studies included atmospheric science, synoptic and physical meteorology, satellite and radar interpretation, numerical weather prediction, severe thunderstorms, tropical meteorology, and forecasting techniques across multiple spatial and temporal scales.

This educational background informs the analysis and interpretation of weather information presented on Apex Wx.

Official Weather Information

Apex Wx is not a substitute for official weather forecasts, watches, warnings, or emergency instructions.

Visitors should consult the appropriate government meteorological agency for authoritative weather information, including:

  • United States: National Weather Service (https://www.weather.gov/)

  • Canada: Environment and Climate Change Canada (https://weather.gc.ca/)

  • Mexico: Servicio Meteorológico Nacional (https://smn.conagua.gob.mx/)

Official forecasts, watches, warnings, and emergency instructions should always take precedence over information presented on Apex Wx.

Severe Weather and Public Safety

During severe or hazardous weather, obtain the latest official warnings and safety information through the National Weather Service, NOAA Weather Radio, Wireless Emergency Alerts, local emergency management agencies, and other reliable communication channels.

Weather conditions can change rapidly, and information published on Apex Wx may not reflect the latest developments.

Never rely exclusively on Apex Wx when making decisions involving personal safety, travel during hazardous weather, property protection, or emergency preparedness.

Forecast Accuracy and Limitations

Weather forecasts are inherently uncertain. Apex Wx forecasts and discussions are developed using available numerical weather prediction models, observational data, personal weather station measurements, and information from the National Weather Service, NOAA, and other meteorological sources.

Forecast accuracy may be affected by atmospheric variability, model limitations, observational errors, equipment malfunctions, delayed data feeds, and other factors beyond the site's control.

Information identified as "current" or "real-time" may be delayed, incomplete, or inaccurate. Unless otherwise indicated, observational data displayed on the website should not be considered independently validated.

Long-range outlooks describe broad temperature and precipitation tendencies rather than precise day-to-day conditions. Their uncertainty generally increases with forecast lead time.
