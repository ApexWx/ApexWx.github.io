The Week Ahead for Fort Kent & the Saint John Valley

November 27 — December 3, 2026

Updated November 27 at 8:05 AM EST
Short Term: Friday, November 27 — Sunday, November 29

The unsettled Thanksgiving holiday pattern continues as a low-pressure system draws warmer, moisture-rich air northward over the colder Canadian air mass established earlier in the week. This thermal contrast supports freezing rain Friday morning as warm air aloft overrides a shallow subfreezing layer near the surface. Sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys may retain low-level cold air longer, potentially extending freezing precipitation into the early afternoon.

As surface temperatures climb above freezing, freezing rain transitions to plain rain, followed by a rain-snow mix as cooler air filters back in on Saturday. Even with little to no measurable ice accumulation, brief freezing rain or patchy freezing drizzle will create slick conditions on untreated roads, bridges, and walkways. Precipitation tapers off through the weekend, though residual surface moisture and overnight cooling introduce localized refreezing concerns Saturday and Sunday nights.

  • Friday: Freezing rain is likely during the morning (80% chance) as warmer air advances over the shallow surface cold layer. Precipitation gradually transitions to plain rain after roughly 1:00 PM as temperatures climb into the upper 30s, though light northeast winds of 2–8 mph will allow cold air to linger longest in sheltered valley bottoms. Cloudy skies and widespread rain continue Friday night (100% chance) with light northeast winds of 3–7 mph, temperatures holding in the mid-30s, and 0.25–0.50 inch of rainfall.

  • Saturday: Lingering rain is likely before 7:00 AM, transitioning to a rain and snow mix through midday (60% chance) as cooler air spreads southward on north winds of 3–8 mph. Additional liquid-equivalent amounts of 0.10–0.25 inch are expected before precipitation tapers off during the afternoon, with daytime highs reaching the lower 40s under persistent cloud cover. A 40% chance of light snow develops after 7:00 PM as temperatures fall into the lower 30s with northwest winds of 1–6 mph, creating a risk for patchy refreezing on wet or slushy surfaces.

  • Sunday: Mostly cloudy and seasonably mild conditions bring a temporary break in active weather. A 30% chance of light snow lingers before 9:00 AM, followed by predominantly dry conditions and only a slight chance of late-afternoon rain as temperatures reach the lower 40s with light south winds up to 3 mph. Mostly cloudy skies persist Sunday night with lows in the lower 30s and south winds increasing to 3–9 mph, ahead of a 20% chance of light rain or snow toward daybreak Monday (under 0.10 inch liquid equivalent).

Medium Range: Monday, November 30 — Thursday, December 3

Another low-pressure system brings widespread rain and snow as November gives way to December, with relatively mild air keeping temperatures above freezing across many areas. Shallow cold air near the surface—particularly in sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys—complicates precipitation type, favoring wet snow where low-level cold air holds or heavier precipitation rates cool the column. While specific rainfall and snowfall totals remain uncertain, a 100% chance of precipitation on Monday signals a high-confidence, widespread event.

As the system departs Monday night, colder air sweeps across northern Maine, creating a strong potential for wet or slushy surfaces to refreeze. High pressure gradually restores drier, seasonably cold weather through midweek, with clearing skies Tuesday night favoring sharp radiational cooling in sheltered valleys. Increasing moisture ahead of another approaching disturbance introduces renewed rain and snow chances by Thursday, though system timing and coverage remain lower confidence.

  • Monday: Widespread rain and snow develop as low pressure tracks across the region (100% chance), accompanied by east winds around 9 mph and highs reaching the lower 40s. Plain rain will dominate in milder spots, while wet snow remains likely where cold air stays trapped in the valleys or within heavier precipitation bands. Rain continues Monday evening before transitioning back to a rain-snow mix after 7:00 PM (80% chance) as colder air filters in overnight. Precipitation tapers off toward 5:00 AM Tuesday as lows drop into the upper 20s, creating slick travel where wet or slushy surfaces refreeze.

  • Tuesday: Conditions improve as the departing storm allows drier, colder air to overspread the Valley. Partly sunny skies and afternoon highs in the mid-30s support dry weather and modest daytime melting on sun-exposed surfaces. Partly cloudy skies continue Tuesday night as lows drop into the upper teens, with locally colder readings in sheltered valley bottoms where light winds and cold-air drainage promote efficient radiational cooling.

  • Wednesday: High pressure delivers mostly sunny skies, dry conditions, and seasonably cold temperatures, with afternoon highs reaching the lower 30s. Clouds increase Wednesday night ahead of the next approaching disturbance, holding overnight lows in the lower 20s—notably milder than Tuesday night due to the insulating cloud cover.

  • Thursday: Mostly cloudy skies and slightly milder air return as moisture increases ahead of the next system, with highs reaching near 40°F. A slight chance of light snow or a rain-snow mix develops during the afternoon (30% chance), with plain rain favored in milder locations and snow possible where colder air persists. Mostly cloudy skies continue Thursday night with lows near 30°F and a lingering chance of rain and snow, though exact timing and precipitation amounts remain track-dependent.
