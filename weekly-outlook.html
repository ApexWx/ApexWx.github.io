Wednesday, November 25 – Friday, November 27

🦃🍂 Happy Thanksgiving from Apex Wx! Wishing you a safe and warm holiday! 🥧🍁

Overview

After temperatures dipped to a frigid 12°F at the Apex Wx weather station in Fort Kent this Wednesday morning, an expanse of Canadian high pressure stretching from New England to Wisconsin will maintain cold, predominantly dry conditions through Thanksgiving Day before a developing low-pressure system draws warmer, moisture-rich air northward. Clear skies and light winds favor substantial radiational cooling—particularly in sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys, where cold-air drainage reinforces the shallow subfreezing layer near the surface.

As high pressure retreats, increasing cloud cover and milder air overriding that trapped surface cold introduce a 40% chance of freezing rain late Thursday night. Precipitation becomes widespread Friday, with a 90% probability and a gradual transition from freezing rain to mixed rain and snow as surface temperatures rise. Although little or no snow or ice accumulation is expected, even brief freezing rain may create slippery untreated surfaces during the morning. The persistence of shallow cold air and timing of precipitation transitions remain important uncertainties for holiday travel.

  • Wednesday: Sunny skies and seasonably cold temperatures prevail as Canadian high pressure maintains dry weather. Afternoon readings reach the lower 30s, with west winds of 1–5 mph making for quiet, favorable conditions ahead of Thanksgiving travel. Clear skies continue Wednesday night, with southwest winds of 0–3 mph and temperatures falling into the upper teens. Substantial radiational cooling may produce locally colder readings in sheltered valley locations where cold air collects overnight.
  • Thanksgiving Day: Mostly sunny skies and slightly milder temperatures provide favorable conditions for daytime holiday travel. Afternoon readings reach the upper 30s before declining toward the lower 30s later in the day, with south winds of 0–3 mph. Increasing cloud cover Thursday night signals an approaching low-pressure system, with temperatures falling into the lower 20s and east winds around 2 mph. A 40% chance of freezing rain develops after 3 AM, with expected liquid precipitation amounts below 0.10 inch. Even light freezing rain could create slippery untreated surfaces early Friday.
  • Friday: Widespread mixed precipitation develops as the approaching low-pressure system spreads warmer air over the colder surface layer. Freezing rain is likely before 9 AM, followed by periods of freezing rain and a slight chance of snow through mid-morning. Precipitation transitions to plain rain or a light rain-snow mix after 10 AM as temperatures rise into the middle 30s. A 90% precipitation probability persists, with northeast winds of 3–8 mph. Little or no snow or ice accumulation is expected, although locally slippery travel conditions remain possible during the morning. Cloudy skies continue Friday night, with widespread rain and snow continuing (90% chance) and temperatures falling into the lower 30s. Northeast winds around 7 mph accompany additional liquid precipitation amounts of 0.10–0.25 inch, with wet or slushy surfaces possible where temperatures approach freezing.

Saturday, November 28 – Tuesday, December 1

Overview

The unsettled Thanksgiving holiday pattern continues into Saturday as a departing low-pressure system maintains lingering moisture and mixed precipitation across the Saint John Valley. Colder air spreading southward gradually shifts rain toward snow, although surface temperatures hovering near freezing complicate precipitation type and limit accumulation potential. Conditions briefly quiet down Sunday as the system weakens, before a secondary disturbance renews rain and snow chances Monday.

The relatively mild air mass supports daytime temperatures above freezing, while nighttime cooling increases the potential for wet or slushy surfaces to refreeze. Sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys may retain colder near-surface air, favoring snow where precipitation persists. As the second disturbance departs, drier air gradually restores quieter conditions. The timing of precipitation transitions and the extent of overnight refreezing remain the primary concerns for travel.

  • Saturday: Rain remains likely early in the morning, transitioning to a mixture of rain and snow between 7 AM and 5 PM as colder air spreads across the region. A 70% chance of precipitation persists, with mostly cloudy skies and temperatures reaching the upper 30s. North winds around 8 mph draw cooler air back into the valley. Wet or slushy roads remain possible where snow develops. Mostly cloudy skies continue Saturday night, with temperatures falling into the upper 20s and a 20% chance of snow after 3 AM. Residual moisture may freeze on untreated surfaces as temperatures drop below freezing
  • Sunday: Mostly cloudy skies and limited precipitation chances continue, with a 20% chance of snow before 10 AM, followed by a slight chance of rain and snow as temperatures reach the upper 30s. Predominantly dry conditions are favored despite lingering atmospheric moisture. Mostly cloudy skies persist Sunday night, with temperatures falling into the lower 30s. A 20% chance of rain and snow develops after 4 AM as a new disturbance approaches.
  • Monday: Rain and snow become likely as a secondary disturbance tracks across the region. A 70% chance of precipitation accompanies mostly cloudy skies and temperatures reaching the lower 40s. Rain becomes more likely where temperatures rise above freezing, although wet snow remains possible in colder locations. Mostly cloudy skies continue Monday night, with a 60% chance of precipitation. Rain is likely before 10 PM, followed by a slight chance of rain and snow through 1 AM. Temperatures fall into the upper 20s, creating the potential for refreezing on untreated surfaces.
  • Tuesday: Partly sunny skies and improving conditions develop as the earlier disturbance moves away. Afternoon temperatures reach the middle 30s, with predominantly dry weather returning. Partly cloudy skies continue Tuesday night, allowing temperatures to fall into the lower 20s. Locally colder readings remain possible in sheltered valley locations where winds diminish and nighttime cooling becomes more efficient.

Long Range Trends: Wednesday, December 2 – Tuesday, December 8

The latest outlook favors above-normal temperatures and precipitation across the SJV into early December. A relatively mild regional air mass and an active storm track support periodic opportunities for rain or snow, with precipitation potentially increasing during passing disturbances. Typical temperatures for this period feature daytime highs in the upper 20s to lower 30s and overnight lows in the mid-teens to lower 20s.

Outlooks updated 7–9 AM weekdays & 8–10 AM weekends and holidays. Long range outlooks indicate broader temperature and precipitation trends rather than precise day-to-day forecasts.
