Overview

Following another frigid start to the day, with temperatures reaching 10°F at the Apex Wx station this morning, Canadian high pressure maintains quiet Thanksgiving weather. An approaching low-pressure system then draws warmer, moisture-rich air northward over the colder air mass established earlier in the week.The resulting temperature contrast supports freezing rain Friday morning, with warmer air aloft overriding a shallow subfreezing layer near the surface. Sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys may retain colder temperatures longer, potentially extending freezing precipitation in low-lying areas.

As surface temperatures rise, freezing rain gradually transitions to ordinary rain. Colder air returning Friday night supports a mixture of rain and snow that lingers into Saturday. Although little or no ice accumulation is expected, even brief freezing rain may create slippery untreated surfaces. Lingering precipitation diminishes Saturday as the system departs, although overnight cooling introduces additional refreezing concerns. The persistence of shallow cold air and timing of precipitation transitions remain the primary uncertainties for holiday travel.

  • Thanksgiving Day: Sunny skies and relatively quiet conditions prevail as Canadian high pressure maintains its influence, providing favorable weather for daytime holiday travel. Afternoon temperatures reach the upper 30s before falling toward 30°F later in the day, with light south winds of 0–3 mph. Increasing cloud cover develops Thursday night as another low-pressure system approaches, with temperatures falling into the lower 20s and east winds around 2 mph. A 30% chance of snow develops after 5 AM Friday. Little or no ice accumulation is expected overnight, although the approaching system introduces more substantial winter-weather concerns for Friday morning.
  • Friday: Freezing rain develops as warmer air advances over the colder surface layer, creating potentially slippery conditions during the morning. A 90% chance of precipitation persists, with freezing rain expected before 10 AM, followed by freezing rain mixed with ordinary rain as temperatures rise into the upper 30s. Northeast winds of 2–8 mph remain relatively light. Little or no ice accumulation is expected, although untreated roads, bridges, and walkways may become slippery. Cloudy skies and widespread precipitation continue Friday night, with a 100% precipitation probability. Rain before 7 PM transitions to rain showers through 1 AM, followed by rain and snow showers as temperatures fall into the lower 30s. Northeast winds around 6 mph accompany additional liquid precipitation amounts of 0.10–0.25 inch.
  • Saturday: Lingering rain and snow showers gradually diminish as the low-pressure system moves away. Showers remain likely before 8 AM, with a chance of additional mixed precipitation through noon. A 60% precipitation probability accompanies mostly cloudy skies and temperatures reaching the upper 30s. North winds of 5–8 mph reinforce the cooler conditions, while additional liquid precipitation amounts remain below 0.10 inch. Improving conditions develop during the afternoon as precipitation becomes less widespread. Cloudy skies continue Saturday night, with temperatures falling into the upper 20s and west winds of 1–7 mph. A 20% chance of snow showers develops after 2 AM. Residual moisture may refreeze on untreated surfaces where temperatures fall below freezing.

Sunday, November 29 – Wednesday, December 2

Overview

The unsettled Thanksgiving holiday pattern continues as another low-pressure system approaches the Saint John Valley, bringing renewed opportunities for widespread rain and snow. Relatively mild air ahead of the disturbance maintains temperatures near or above freezing, complicating precipitation type as atmospheric moisture increases late Sunday night. A 100% precipitation probability Monday indicates widespread rain and snow, although precipitation amounts and the distribution of rain versus snow remain uncertain.

Sheltered portions of the Saint John and Fish River valleys may retain colder near-surface air, supporting wet snow where precipitation persists or becomes heavier. As the system departs Monday night, colder air spreads across the region, increasing the potential for wet or slushy surfaces to refreeze. High pressure gradually restores drier, more seasonable conditions through midweek, with nighttime cooling becoming more pronounced. The primary uncertainties involve precipitation type, accumulation amounts, and potential travel impacts.

  • Sunday: Mostly cloudy skies and relatively mild temperatures provide a temporary break from the unsettled Thanksgiving weather. Afternoon readings reach the lower 40s, with southwest winds of 1–6 mph and predominantly dry conditions. Mostly cloudy skies continue Sunday night, with temperatures falling to around 32°F. A slight chance of rain develops between 3 and 4 AM, followed by a 30% chance of rain and snow as another low-pressure system approaches. Precipitation type remains sensitive to small temperature differences near freezing.
  • Monday: Widespread rain and snow develop as the approaching disturbance spreads moisture across the region. Cloudy skies and a 100% precipitation probability accompany temperatures reaching the lower 40s. Rain becomes more prevalent where temperatures rise above freezing, although wet snow remains possible in colder locations and during heavier precipitation. Snowfall and rainfall amounts remain uncertain. Mostly cloudy skies continue Monday night, with a 60% chance of precipitation. Rain remains likely before 7 PM, followed by a chance of rain and snow through 2 AM. Temperatures fall into the upper 20s, creating the potential for slippery untreated surfaces as residual moisture freezes
  • Tuesday: Partly sunny skies and colder temperatures prevail as the departing disturbance allows drier air to spread across the region. Afternoon readings reach the middle 30s, with predominantly dry conditions returning. Partly cloudy skies continue Tuesday night, allowing temperatures to fall into the lower 20s. Sheltered portions of the Valley may experience locally colder readings where winds diminish and nighttime cooling becomes more efficient.
  • Wednesday: Mostly sunny skies and seasonably cold conditions continue as high pressure maintains predominantly dry weather. Afternoon temperatures reach the middle 30s, providing favorable conditions for outdoor activities. Mostly cloudy skies develop Wednesday night, with temperatures falling into the middle 20s. No significant precipitation is indicated through the overnight period.

Long Range Trends: Thursday, December 3 – Wednesday, December 9

Long-range trends favor above-normal temperatures and precipitation across the Saint John Valley into early December. A relatively mild air mass and an active storm track across the Northeast support periodic opportunities for rain, snow, or mixed precipitation as disturbances move through the region. Typical temperatures for this period feature daytime highs in the upper 20s to lower 30s and overnight lows in the mid-teens to lower 20s.
