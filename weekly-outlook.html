Fort Kent & the Saint John Valley of Maine

Short-Term: Saturday, November 28 — Monday, November 30

Lingering moisture behind the departing Thanksgiving storm supports scattered rain and snow showers today before weak high pressure brings a brief stretch of quieter weather to close out the holiday weekend. Daytime temperatures hovering in the upper 30s will keep precipitation mixed, while tonight's drop below freezing creates a risk for patchy black ice on untreated roads and walkways. Attention then turns to an approaching upper-level trough driving a strengthening low-pressure system northeastward across Maine. Widespread rain develops Monday—with totals approaching an inch—before a sharp cold front sweeps through Monday night. Rapidly falling temperatures, a transition to light snow, and gusty northwest winds raise concerns for slick travel, downed branches, and isolated power outages, particularly across higher and exposed terrain. The storm's exact track will dictate peak wind gusts and how quickly rain changes over to snow.

  • Saturday: Lingering morning rain and snow transition to scattered mixed showers through late afternoon as the departing system pulls away. Skies remain cloudy with highs in the upper 30s and a north wind at 5–8 mph; additional liquid amounts stay under 0.10 inch (70% chance of precipitation). Cloudy skies persist tonight with lows in the upper 20s and a northwest wind of 1–7 mph, allowing residual moisture to refreeze on untreated surfaces.
  • Sunday: A brief break between systems brings dry weather for holiday return travel, though mostly cloudy skies linger. Afternoon highs reach the upper 30s with light west winds under 5 mph. Clouds thicken Sunday night as south winds increase to 3–9 mph and temperatures dip into the lower 30s, with a slight chance of rain (20%) arriving toward daybreak Monday.
  • Monday: A strengthening low-pressure system brings widespread, steady rain and building winds. Highs reach the lower 40s with an east wind of 8–13 mph gusting to 23 mph, and rainfall totals of 0.75–1.00 inch may reduce visibility and create ponding on roadways (100% chance of precipitation). Rain continues into the evening before changing over to snow after 11 PM as colder air surges in (80% chance of precipitation). Overnight lows fall into the mid-20s as northwest winds ramp up to 9–22 mph with gusts near 40 mph. While snow accumulation will stay under a half-inch, wet roads will rapidly freeze over.

Medium-Range: Tuesday, December 1 — Friday, December 4

Blustery northwest winds and brisk Canadian air dominate the start of the week in the wake of Monday's storm, generating bitter wind chills and lingering icy spots on untreated surfaces Tuesday morning. High pressure then settles over northern Maine through midweek, easing the wind and setting the stage for crisp, sunny days and sharp nocturnal radiational cooling—especially in sheltered pockets of the Saint John and Fish River valleys. As the high shifts east late Thursday, milder air and returning moisture introduce the next chance for precipitation. Because shallow cold air often lingers near the valley floor, a wintry mix of rain and snow is possible Thursday afternoon and night before drier weather returns to end the week.

  • Tuesday: Mostly sunny skies return, but brisk northwest winds of 15–22 mph—gusting up to 38 mph—keep wind chills noticeably cold despite afternoon highs in the lower 30s. Little to no additional snow is expected, though untreated wet surfaces will remain icy. Partly cloudy skies and diminishing winds Tuesday night allow temperatures to drop into the mid-teens, with locally colder readings in sheltered valleys.
  • Wednesday: High pressure delivers abundant sunshine, lighter winds, and seasonably cold highs in the lower 30s. Clouds increase Wednesday night ahead of the next approaching disturbance, holding overnight lows in the lower 20s.
  • Thursday: Milder air surges northward ahead of an approaching front, pushing afternoon highs into the lower 40s under mostly cloudy skies. A 30% chance of rain and snow develops after 2 PM, with precipitation type depending on how quickly shallow cold air erodes at the surface. Mostly cloudy skies continue Thursday night with lows in the lower 30s and a 40% chance of mixed rain and snow; significant accumulation is not currently indicated.
  • Friday: A slight chance of early-morning rain (20%) tapers off before 7 AM as the disturbance exits, giving way to partly sunny, dry conditions and highs in the upper 30s. Clouds rebuild Friday night with lows in the upper 20s and a slight chance of light snow returning after 1 AM Saturday.
