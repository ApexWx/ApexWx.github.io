Thanksgiving Storm Gradually Departs as Heavy Mountain Snow Develops in the Northwest

The massive Thanksgiving storm slowly weakens Friday after bringing damaging winds and heavy rain along the East Coast and up to 15 inches of snow in eastern Missouri. Freezing rain threatens northern New England, with ice accumulation reaching 0.20 inch in some locations. Wet snow extends into the Tennessee Valley and higher elevations of the central Appalachians, while cold rain continues across the Midwest, Ohio Valley, and lower Great Lakes.

Blustery winds of 25–35 mph persist across the East Friday before conditions improve Saturday. The Southeast experiences freezing morning temperatures, followed by moderating afternoon temperatures reaching the 60s.

Meanwhile, another storm brings rain to coastal Oregon and additional mountain snow across the Pacific Northwest. Up to 6 inches of snow is possible in the Cascades. Renewed Pacific moisture spreads into the northern Rockies, producing more than a foot of snow in some Montana and Wyoming mountain passes, with wind gusts exceeding 50 mph.

Dry, relatively mild weather continues across the central United States before rain develops over the central Plains Saturday, spreading toward the Midwest and Mississippi Valley by Sunday morning.
