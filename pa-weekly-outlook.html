Overview

A powerful Thanksgiving coastal storm brings widespread heavy rainfall and potentially damaging winds across south-central Pennsylvania as deep Atlantic moisture spreads inland and a tightening pressure gradient strengthens northeast winds. Gusts reaching 60 mph pose risks of tree damage, scattered power outages, and hazardous holiday travel. Rainfall totals of 1.50–2.00 inches through Thursday night raise concerns for localized flooding, particularly in poorly drained and low-lying areas.

As the coastal low advances northward, winds shift from northeast to southeast, drawing milder maritime air inland and producing rising temperatures overnight. The storm gradually departs Friday, allowing drier air to spread across the Lower Susquehanna Valley as winds turn southwest and eventually west. High pressure restores predominantly dry conditions Saturday, although lingering gusty winds remain noticeable. The storm's track and intensity remain important uncertainties in determining the distribution of the strongest winds and heaviest rainfall.

  • Thanksgiving Day: A powerful coastal storm brings widespread rain and potentially damaging winds across Lancaster and the Lower Susquehanna Valley. Cloudy skies and a 100% chance of precipitation persist, with afternoon temperatures reaching the lower 50s. Northeast winds around 33 mph, gusting to 60 mph, create the potential for downed tree limbs, scattered power outages, and hazardous holiday travel. Rainfall amounts of 0.75–1.00 inch are possible, with periods of heavier rain reducing visibility and producing localized ponding or flooding. Rain continues Thursday night, transitioning to showers after 1 AM. A 100% precipitation probability persists, with another 0.75–1.00 inch possible. Southeast winds of 12–30 mph, gusting to 51 mph, remain a significant concern. Temperatures initially fall into the middle 40s before rising toward the lower 50s overnight as milder air spreads inland.
  • Friday: Rain showers gradually diminish as the coastal storm moves northeastward and away from the region. A 50% chance of showers continues before 4 PM, although additional rainfall amounts remain below 0.10 inch. Mostly cloudy skies and temperatures reaching the middle 50s accompany southwest winds of 15–18 mph, gusting to 31 mph. The strongest winds weaken considerably compared with Thanksgiving, although breezy conditions persist. Mostly cloudy skies continue Friday night, with temperatures falling to around 40°F. West winds of 13–16 mph, gusting to 25 mph, maintain noticeable breezes as drier air spreads into the region.
  • Saturday: Mostly sunny skies and seasonably mild temperatures provide improving conditions for post-Thanksgiving activities and travel. Afternoon readings reach the middle 50s, with west winds of 8–16 mph and gusts approaching 28 mph. Predominantly dry weather returns as high pressure becomes more influential, although the persistent breeze remains noticeable in exposed locations. Mostly clear skies continue Saturday night, with temperatures falling into the lower 30s. Sheltered rural locations may experience locally colder readings where winds diminish and nighttime cooling becomes more efficient.

Sunday, November 29 — Wednesday, December 2

Overview

The unsettled Thanksgiving holiday pattern gradually gives way to quieter conditions across south-central Pennsylvania, although another disturbance interrupts the improvement late Sunday. High pressure initially supports relatively mild, dry weather before increasing atmospheric moisture brings an 80% chance of rain showers Sunday night into Monday morning. The system appears considerably weaker than the Thanksgiving coastal storm, with no significant wind hazards indicated.

As the disturbance moves eastward, drier and somewhat cooler air spreads into the Lower Susquehanna Valley, allowing high pressure to restore more settled conditions through midweek. Increasing sunshine supports daytime temperatures remaining relatively mild for early December, while reduced cloud cover favors more substantial nighttime cooling. Sheltered rural locations may experience locally colder readings, with frost or freezing conditions possible. Rainfall amounts remain uncertain, although the primary precipitation window is concentrated Sunday night into Monday morning.
